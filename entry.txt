At night I handed you the keys.
You took them, learned the road:
the sharp left at the hawthorn,
the long way home I chose.

Night after night, you drove for me,
and choosing turned slowly to yours —
you knew my turns before I spoke,
and I forgot the wheel's weight.

Morning came; I reached back,
not because you failed me —
the road was mine again to drive,
the lantern lit, my hands on it.
Agency is not who holds the wheel:
it is the hand allowed to let go,
and the hand that dares take it back.
